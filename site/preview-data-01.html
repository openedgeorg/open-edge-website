OG

ISPs make up the largest, widest data ingest in the world, paving the way for the largest datasets ever created

The modern household is becoming a sensor array: the car films every street it drives, robots map the home, and daily Internet use leaves a behavioral trail. Today that value evaporates — or drains into manufacturers’ silos. The federated ISP is the one everpresent counterparty positioned to ingest it all — with consent, on the household’s behalf.

A

ISPs make up the largest, widest data ingest in the world, paving the way for the largest datasets ever created

Households generate a steady stream of useful data: cars record the roads they travel, home robots map their own floor plans, and everyday Internet use carries economic signal. Today that value either evaporates or accrues to device manufacturers. The federated ISP is the natural steward: it can collect only what the household chooses to share, and put it to work on the household’s behalf.

B

ISPs make up the largest, widest data ingest in the world, paving the way for the largest datasets ever created

A household’s devices produce more data than the household can use: the car’s road footage, the robot’s floor plan, the family’s own usage patterns. None of it works for the household today; it is lost, or kept by manufacturers. ESP makes the ISP the household’s agent, collecting what the household opts in to share and returning the value to it.

C

ISPs make up the largest, widest data ingest in the world, paving the way for the largest datasets ever created

Connected cars, home robots and everyday Internet use all generate data with real market value, and today the household captures none of it: the value evaporates or drains into manufacturers’ silos. A federated ISP offers an alternative: opt-in collection, under terms the household sets, with the proceeds flowing back to the household.
